Five kinds of proof

Every width carries its source, and the strongest one decides. A tape measure beats the city cadastre, the cadastre beats a listing photo, and a guess never overrules anything.

A clear width taken on the ground with a tape or a phone, at the alley's narrowest point, by someone standing in it. It overrules every other source.

Collected with the field kit, reviewed before it counts.

The gap between land parcels in the city's cadastral map, sampled at cuts along the alley. Legal boundaries, not obstacles, so a parked row of motorbikes still counts against it.

216 alleys with city cadastral widths.

A width read from a dated photograph of the alley itself, such as a property listing or a street camera, against a known object in the frame.

13,018 ways with an observed width.

What a listing says about its own street: "hẻm xe hơi", "hẻm 3 m". Useful, often generous, never decisive on its own.

1,789 ways where a declaration is the strongest source.

An estimate from the geometry of buildings and parcels around the street. Shown with its range, and the answer stays unverified until something stronger arrives.

207 ways where only an inference exists.
